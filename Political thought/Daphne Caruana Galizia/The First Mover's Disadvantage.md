The PN Attempted to strike the iron while it was hot. In principle this sounded like a strategic must, to ride the media criticism, but there are a few faults with this reasoning:

1. The door is open to critique.
	1. If you move first, the opponent will move after you, and one of his moves can be to discredit your previous statements, critique you or even twist your statements against you, as we have seen in parliament, the news and most labour media. If the party opted to wait, let the NGOs speak first AND let government make their first move, the roles would be switched. 
2. News works in cycles.
	1. The best interest of the party is to prolong the emotion, outrage and people's understanding. If, the party exhausts all options at once, at the start and breaks point 1, sure, you have a strong sudden burst, but one which may be forgotten
3. Statements are likely rushed.
	1. Naturally, moving first means moving quick, and quick likely means rushed.

Beyond these faults related to First Mover's Disadvantage, PN also made a very foolish decision, they relied on **assumptions**. How can you say that the murder was planned in Kastille?  How can you say that certain government officials were complicit in the murder when the public inquiry disproved it (but mentioned a culture of impunity)?

This will alienate the labour-leaning core, and also give room to the enemy to critique, and critique they did.
